# Client meeting and proposed engagement

Status: Consultant working guide. Proposed terms and steps have not been accepted by the client.

## Positioning

Offer defined technology and administrative support delivered by one consultant. The consultant can assess needs across departments, but the owner retains business decisions and the relevant professionals retain responsibility for legal, accounting, and employment advice.

The first demonstration concerns internal administrative records and responsibilities. Cannabis promotion, ordering, sales, and delivery systems are outside the proposed work described here.

## What we know

The consultant has spoken with the client and reports an existing shop using paper records and a card-payment terminal. The client wants broader help than a website. No detailed administrative stories, verified baseline, budget, or formal scope have been supplied. The consultant prefers a project fee plus a monthly service fee.

## Meeting guide

### Establish the owner's priorities

1. What would make this engagement worthwhile to you over the first few months?
2. Which administrative activity takes more time than it should? Walk through the most recent example.
3. Who performs that activity, and who checks the result?
4. What happens when that person is away?
5. Which records are hardest to find when you need them?

### Understand the existing environment

6. What devices, internet access, email accounts, and software are already available?
7. Who owns the accounts and can authorise access or changes?
8. What administrative information exists on paper, in spreadsheets, or in messages?
9. What should remain private, and who should be able to see it?
10. Is there an accountant or other adviser whose requirements an administrative tool must respect?

### Test the demonstration's assumptions

11. Which demonstrated activity resembles work you actually do?
12. Which part would you remove or change?
13. Who would enter the information, and when would they do it?
14. What would stop staff from using this consistently?
15. How could we measure whether one administrative improvement helped?

### Establish authority and commercial scope

16. Who approves the work, costs, content, and eventual handover?
17. What budget range and timing should a proposal work within?
18. What ongoing availability do you expect from the consultant?
19. What documents establish the business's relevant authorisations, and who can obtain qualified advice on their scope?
20. What would you expect to own or retain if the engagement ended?

Do not request sensitive personal records for the first demo. Record authorisation questions as unresolved until documentation and appropriate professional review establish the position. Do not make licensing or compliance claims on the client's behalf.

## Proposed commercial stages

| Stage | Defined output | Commercial treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial demonstration | Fictional prototype and discussion | Consultant's bounded pre-sales effort; no agreed fee recorded |
| Discovery | Current-state notes, prioritised administrative needs, access/ownership inventory, proposed pilot and estimate | Proposed separately priced engagement |
| Pilot implementation | One agreed administrative workflow, suitable production controls, training, and handover | Project fee against agreed milestones |
| Ongoing service | Named maintenance and support services, scheduled reporting, and a defined improvement allowance if agreed | Monthly service fee |

The pilot should be selected after discovery. The prototype's three functional areas do not automatically become three production commitments. Production access controls, storage, backups, privacy requirements, and deployment require their own scoped decisions.

## Proposal checklist

- Define the deliverables and exclusions in plain language.
- State how acceptance will be assessed and who signs off.
- List client dependencies, such as access, accurate information, timely feedback, and nominated staff.
- Specify project milestones, payment terms, third-party costs, and treatment of changes.
- Define monthly included services and hours, support windows, request channels, and escalation arrangements.
- Distinguish response time from resolution time; do not imply continuous availability.
- State whether unused hours carry over and how additional work is quoted.
- Specify ownership of accounts, data, code, and documentation, together with exit and handover arrangements.
- Obtain appropriate professional review of contractual or legal terms where needed.
- Avoid guaranteeing profit growth or charging against an unverified profit baseline.

No fee amounts are recommended yet. Price against actual effort, responsibilities, costs, and client scope after discovery.

## Capacity assumptions

Reported availability is two to four hours each weekday and eight hours on weekends. Provisionally interpret the weekend figure as a total: 18–28 hours per week. Confirm this interpretation before making commitments.

That time also includes discovery, meetings, documentation, and administration. Do not sell all of it as uninterrupted implementation or guaranteed reactive support. The consultant must choose a sustainable client allocation when pricing the engagement.

## Evidence of pilot value

Select one observable measure during discovery and record a baseline before implementation. Possible administrative measures include time to retrieve a document, the proportion of tasks with a named owner, or completeness of expense records. These are candidate measures, not established client problems or promised results.

## Decisions still open

- The client's highest-priority administrative problem and whether this demo addresses it.
- Business identity, approved visual assets, stakeholders, budget, and timing.
- Production data, user access, record retention, hosting, and support requirements.
- Documented authorisations and the advice required to understand their scope.
- Project and monthly service pricing, contractual terms, and consultant availability.
- Whether the eventual repository represents this client engagement or a wider consultancy.
