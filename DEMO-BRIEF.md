# Deferred administration demonstration

This was the original demonstration direction. The public cultural information website in `PUBLIC-SITE.md` now takes priority; keep this brief for a possible later administration phase.

## Purpose

Give the prospective client a concrete example of the consultant's ability to organise an administrative process and maintain the supporting technology. Use the demonstration to open a discussion about a paid, defined engagement. It is not evidence of improved profits or a finished production product.

## Audience and working premise

Primary audience: the shop owner or person who can approve the engagement. Their identity and decision-making authority are not yet established.

Working premise: an organised view of tasks, expenses, and paperwork may be useful to a business using paper records and a standalone card terminal. This premise must be tested in conversation.

## Visual direction

Use the placeholder name "Business Workspace". Aim for a clean, confident internal tool: warm light background, dark readable text, one restrained accent colour, generous spacing, and clear status labels. Make the interface work comfortably on a phone as well as a laptop.

Show "Demo — fictional data" persistently. A reference date and a Reset demo control make the scenario repeatable. Avoid cannabis imagery, product promotions, invented logos, fake financial performance charts, and unsupported compliance badges.

## Four views

| View | Main content | Working interaction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overview | Open and overdue tasks, paid and unpaid administrative expenses, document reviews | Open the underlying records |
| Tasks | Responsibility, due date, status, notes | Create, assign, update, complete, filter |
| Expenses | Date, description, category, amount, payment status | Add a fictional expense and link it to a task |
| Documents | Fictional equipment and administrative records | Search titles and preview sample content |

No inactive buttons suggesting unimplemented features. No real sign-in or simulated security claims. If an interaction is future work, describe it verbally rather than disguising it as functional software.

## Demonstration story: receipt-printer fault

Use fictional people and an explicitly fictional R250.00 repair cost.

1. Open Overview and explain what the sample information represents.
2. Record "Receipt printer intermittently disconnects" as a task, with a description and a due date.
3. Assign it to the sample consultant and change it to In progress.
4. Record the sample repair expense and link it to the task.
5. Add a completion note, mark the task Done, and show the updated overview.
6. Search for the sample printer warranty and open its contents.
7. Ask the owner whether this resembles a useful process in their business, and what they would change.

Target a five-minute demonstration. Keep discussion time separate. The story demonstrates information flow; it makes no claim about the client's actual equipment or problems.

## Suggested opening

"I've built a small example of how administrative work could be organised. All the information is fictional. I want to use it to understand which parts, if any, would be useful to your business before we agree on an implementation."

## Suggested close

"The next step would be a defined discovery engagement: review your current processes and technology, identify one useful first improvement, and give you a clear scope and cost. Implementation would have a project fee, with ongoing services agreed separately under a monthly service fee."

## Presentation preparation

- Check the full scenario on a laptop and a phone-width viewport.
- Restore the original sample records before the meeting.
- Keep the demonstration independent of external accounts and live APIs.
- Bring the distinction between prototype capabilities and future production requirements into the conversation.
- Record feedback as observations, requests, or unanswered questions; do not silently convert interest into agreed scope.

## What success means at this stage

The owner understands the demonstration, identifies whether the proposed administrative workflow is relevant, and agrees on a concrete next commercial discussion. A visually impressive response alone is not validation of the proposed system.
